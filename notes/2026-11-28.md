## Executive Summary
**Fact:** Before the owner-approved reweighting later on 28 Nov (jobs report, CPI and dot plot weigh more), model odds were bear 63.6% / base 31.4% / bull 5.0%; after it they are bear 59.6% / base 35.4% / bull 5.0%. The earlier figures moved (from 58.3 / 36.7 / 5.0 on 2026-11-24). The model attributes the shift to the inflation group, after October core PCE (released this week) came in at 3.26% y/y and 3.63% 3-month annualised (PCEPILFE, Oct 2026).
**Interpretation:** This week's move comes from one lagging data point. The leading inflation gauges point the other way: 5-year breakeven 2.16% (T5YIE, 2026-11-27), down 0.30pp in 4 weeks, and WTI $82.24 (2026-11-24), down 20% in 4 weeks. Credit (HY +114bp in 13 weeks to 3.77%, 2026-11-26) is still the main bear driver. No override applied. Holiday week, thin data.

## Liquidity Update
**Fact:** Market liquidity -0.47 z (from -0.36). Fed net liquidity +$50bn over 13 weeks (H.4.1, 2026-11-25). Global liquidity -1.09 (ECB+BoJ −$345bn over 13 weeks, about two-thirds from currency moves). Private credit impulse turned from +0.16 to -0.17.
**Interpretation:** The Fed is not draining. The drag comes from abroad and from credit. A strong dollar (broad index +4.7% over 3 months, DTWEXBGS 2026-11-20) shrinks foreign balance sheets in dollar terms. Economy liquidity (+0.73) still supports growth 2–4 quarters out. Market liquidity is the measure that has led stocks, and it is drifting lower but is not yet clearly negative (below -0.5).

## Central Banks
**Fact:** Fed funds 3.88% (DFF, 2026-11-26). Real rate +0.62% using core PCE 3.26% (Oct). 1-year yield 4.56% (2026-11-25), which prices about 0.68pp of tightening over a year. 2-year 4.61%. ECB deposit rate 2.50% (2026-11-27).
**Interpretation:** Hot core PCE strengthens the case for another hike at the 2026-12-09 FOMC. But the 2-year yield fell 6bp in 4 weeks while bills rose. Markets may be starting to doubt that the Fed can go much further if credit keeps widening. That is the 'Fed boxed in' setup in the bear case.

## Consumer and Employment
**Fact:** Unemployment 4.1% (UNRATE, Oct 2026), -0.3pp y/y. Claims 4-week average 196k (ICSA, 2026-11-21), at its 6-month low. Saving rate 4.1% (PSAVERT, Oct), -0.8pp y/y. Real wages -0.6% y/y (wages +3.1% vs CPI +3.7%).
**Interpretation:** Jobs are strong and argue against the bear case's 'unemployment rises' leg. The weak spot is how spending is funded: falling saving and negative real wages leave little cushion. Delinquency data are from 2026 Q2 and are stale for this question.

## Economic Cycle
**Fact:** Phase expansion; reflation quadrant. Leading +0.25 z, coincident +0.05. Curve-based recession odds 16.8%. Equipment orders +1.34 z, housing permits -0.97 z (both Oct 2026).
**Interpretation:** There is a gap between the cycle reading (expansion, low recession odds) and the scenario model (bear 64% before reweighting, 60% after). The bear odds are driven by credit and valuation, not by activity. Growth data alone do not support a near-term downturn. The risk is a financial shock first, then the economy, which matches the research finding that credit leads.

Separately, implied volatility is below realised (-4.2 pts), which means protection is cheap, not 'pricey' as the compensation table says. Both labels need a code fix. ## Investment Implications
**Fact:** Framework favours cash (+0.66) and commodities (+0.23). It is least keen on IG credit (-1.45) and international equities (-1.36). Gold is -18.2% over 3 months while the 10-year real yield rose 39bp to 2.73% (2026-11-25).
**Interpretation:** Leadership is defensive (staples, health care, utilities) and away from growth (growth vs value -11.1pp over 3 months). That is consistent with the framework. Long Treasuries are now 'well paid' (term premium 0.61%), so bonds are the main hedge to watch if credit cracks. Hot inflation keeps them only neutral for now.

## Forward Outlook
**Interpretation:** The bear case is the model's lead, but none of its own triggers has fired yet: HY 3.77% vs the 5.3% trigger, claims 196k vs 227k, Sahm below 0.3. The next two weeks matter: the 2026-12-07 jobs report, the 2026-12-09 FOMC and 2026-12-14 CPI. A softer CPI plus falling breakevens would undo most of this week's shift. Further HY widening toward 4.5% would confirm it.
